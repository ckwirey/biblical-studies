# λόγος (Logos)

## Greek Term
**λόγος** — Transliterated as *logos*. Commonly translated as "word," "reason," or "principle."

## Lexical Meaning
- Classical Greek: a reasoned statement, speech, or principle
- Hellenistic Jewish usage: divine wisdom, agent of creation
- New Testament usage: particularly in John 1:1, where it carries both philosophical and theological weight

## Historical and Textual Context
- In **John 1:1**, John writes:  
  *"In the beginning was the Word (λόγος), and the Word was with God, and the Word was God."*
- Greek philosophy used λόγος to denote rationality, principle, or ordering reason in the universe.
- Jewish Hellenistic writers used λόγος to describe divine wisdom acting in creation or in the world.
- John’s usage combines these strands: he portrays the Logos as preexistent, divine, and intimately involved in creation and revelation.
- Early Christian interpreters (Church Fathers) recognized the philosophical resonance while emphasizing the theological claim: the Logos is God incarnate in Christ.

## Theological Implications
- Shows how John merges Jewish and Hellenistic concepts to communicate Jesus’ divinity
- Demonstrates that “Word” is not just speech but a **creative, rational, and relational principle**
- Central to understanding Christology in Johannine thought
